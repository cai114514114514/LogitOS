HOW FAR IS LogitOS's PAGE RECLAIM FROM THE OFFLINE OPTIMUM?
A measurement taken 2026-08-09. Reproduce with tests/mmtrace.mk.

WHAT KIND OF NUMBER THIS IS
===========================
An EXACT gap on a recorded reference string, not a bound and not a sample.
Every user-space page reference of each run was captured by a QEMU TCG plugin
(tools/mmtrace/mmtrace.c) and replayed against six replacement policies,
including Belady's MIN computed exactly (tools/mmtrace/mmsim.c). No kernel
change was involved, so the traced machine is the machine.

What the number is NOT: it is not a prediction of how the kernel would behave
under a different policy, because a different policy changes WHEN pages are
faulted and therefore changes timing. It is the standard, and the only exactly
computable, formulation: given this reference string and this much memory, how
many misses does each policy take.

Four caveats, all of them real:
  - Recorded at -smp 1. A reference string is an ORDER, and several vCPUs do
    not produce one. The cost is that reclaim.c's skip_busy case (a frame in a
    space another core is running) cannot occur and is not measured.
  - The browser traces stopped at the record cap (400M / 350M records). Both
    had already painted the page. The strings are shorter than the runs.
  - The simulation is over ONE address space (the workload's). The window
    manager and the shell are two orders of magnitude smaller and are excluded.
  - Frame identity: the simulator models pmm.c's next-fit allocation and
    reclaim.c's ascending frame sweep, so sweep order stands in the same
    relation to allocation order that it does on the machine. It does not model
    the two tiers -- a miss is a miss, whether the kernel would have re-derived
    the page for free or read it off the swap device.

THE CONTROLS (make test-mmsim) -- ALL THREE PASS
================================================
1. Sequential scan of N+1 pages through N frames, N=100:
     lru/fifo/clock  5050 misses of 5050 references   (miss rate 1.0000)
     MIN              150 misses                       (miss rate 0.0297)
     -> 101x. The textbook answer, and the proof MIN is right.
2. Uniform random, 2000 pages through 10 frames:
     every policy within 1.08x of MIN. No policy signal where there is no
     locality, as required.
3. Hot set inside a scan (1000 pages, hot 50, 100 frames), capacity misses:
     lru 239315  <  clock 249868  <  fifo 264215
     -> the clock sits strictly between LRU and FIFO, which is what a
        second-chance clock IS. A model of it accidentally equal to LRU would
        have passed controls 1 and 2.

THREE THINGS THE TABLE SAYS
===========================
1. THE GAP ON REAL WORKLOADS IS ABOUT 2x TO 3x, and it is almost entirely
   LRU-to-MIN, not clock-to-LRU. clk/lru is 1.00-1.12 everywhere, typically
   1.04. Better recency tracking -- which is what most "learn the replacement
   policy" work amounts to -- is worth a few percent here. The 2-3x needs
   PREDICTION of future reuse, which is the thing MIN has and no online policy
   does.

2. THE WATERMARK BATCHING COSTS MORE THAN THE CHOICE OF CLOCK OVER LRU.
   wm/clk is the same clock with reclaim.c's batching (evict nothing until free
   < total/32, then take a batch up to total/16) against the same clock evicting
   one frame on demand. It is +1% to +13% in the meaningful band and +20% in one
   case -- consistently larger than the 4% the clock gives up to true LRU. That
   is a tuning question, not a policy question, and it needs no model.

3. ON A SEQUENTIAL SCAN THE CLOCK IS 10x OFF AND RANDOM EVICTION BEATS IT.
   mempress at 90%: clock 49193, MIN 4871, random 8446. Every page misses
   exactly twice under clock and LRU alike, because both evict precisely the
   page about to be read back. This is the classic pathology, and it matters
   here because mempress is the swap test's own workload: the kernel's headline
   memory-pressure test is the case its policy is worst at.

WHERE THE CLOCK LOSES (shape)
=============================
  browser (wikipedia, 10% of footprint, 20 equal windows):
    ratio 1.27-2.35x through parse and layout, then 3.40x and 3.06x in the two
    windows where the JS engine is compiling and running. Bursty, and the burst
    is the scripting phase.
    Of the 244811 references the clock missed and MIN did not:
      7110 code (2.9%), 2727 written data (1.1%), 234974 read-only data (96%).
    Read-only data is the browser's decoded-and-parsed resources, and evicting
    them just before reuse is where the whole gap is.

  video (10%): flat at 1.93-2.07x across every window -- steady-state decode
    with no phase structure at all. The breakdown is different from the
    browser's: 12202 code (39%), 4387 written data, 14768 read-only data. A
    small decoder loop hammering reference frames evicts its own text.

  mempress (75%): windows 0-4 are compulsory. Window 5, where the read-back
    pass begins, is 10.48x; windows 6-8 the clock takes 4057 misses and MIN
    takes 0. A pure phase change that the clock cannot see coming.

DOES THE MODEL MATCH THE MACHINE?
=================================
A real `run-swap-test.sh swap 192 mid` (the same mempress, on a 192 MiB machine
with an NVMe swap device, reclaim actually running) reported:

  total=49109 frames  evicted=62162 (dropped 36316 + swapped 25846)
  swapin=24603  second=73264  bugs=0

The data region is 24576 pages and swapin was 24603 -- every data page went out
and came back exactly once, which is precisely the shape the simulated clock
predicts (each page missing once beyond its compulsory miss; 49193 capacity
misses at 75-90% of footprint). The machine's 60919 re-faults against the
model's 49193 is 24% high, and the excess is what the simulation excludes: the
AetherScript interpreter's own working set, the window manager, and the
batching evicting past what was needed. Same order, same shape.

At that operating point MIN would have taken 12259 capacity misses instead of
49193. The run moved 25846 pages out and 24603 back over a polled NVMe queue;
an optimal policy moves about a quarter of that.

WHEN ANY OF THIS MATTERS ON THIS MACHINE
========================================
Only under manufactured pressure. The browser's whole footprint is 18-26 MiB
and the full desktop peaks at 229 MiB of 511; at any real operating point
memory is far larger than the working set, MIN's capacity misses are ZERO and
so are almost everybody else's, and reclaim never runs. The 2-3x is real and it
is what a better policy could win, but it is a gap that only opens when the
machine is deliberately made too small.

WHAT MEASURING COST
===================
  tracing overhead   /bin/vidcheck end to end: 40.7 s untraced, 46.7 s traced
                     (+14.8%), both at -smp 1 TCG. The plugin reads CR3 once
                     per emitted record, not per access.
  trace size         16 bytes per page-crossing reference.
                     video     89.6M refs = 1.43 GB for ~15 s of decoding
                     mempress 290.4M refs = 4.65 GB
                     wikipedia 400.0M refs = 6.40 GB (hit the cap)
                     kimi      350.0M refs = 5.60 GB (hit the cap)
  simulation         all six policies over 400M references: 77 s, 3.2 GB RSS.
                     Memory is 8 bytes per reference (the id sequence and the
                     next-use array), which is what bounds trace length.
